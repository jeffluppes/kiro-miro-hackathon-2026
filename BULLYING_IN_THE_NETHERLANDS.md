# Bullying, School Safety, and Citizenship Education in the Netherlands (2024–2025)

## Executive Summary

Recent Dutch education and anti-bullying reports show a consistent upward trend in bullying-related incidents, psychosocial violence, and social safety concerns in schools. At the same time, schools are under increasing pressure to strengthen citizenship education (“burgerschapsonderwijs”) and social cohesion.

Several indicators point to structural stress factors:

- Rising bullying and psychosocial violence reports in primary and secondary schools
- Increasing workload and emotional burden for teachers
- Growing concerns around polarization, discrimination, and unsafe school climates
- Budget pressure on social support systems and youth services
- Insufficiently structured citizenship education at many schools

The Dutch Inspectorate of Education and multiple NGOs increasingly connect these issues to weakening social infrastructure and reduced preventive support capacity.

---

# Key Numbers & Trends

## Bullying in Schools

### Primary Education (Basisschool)

- Approximately **154,000 children** in Dutch primary education experience bullying annually.
- Long-term and severe bullying is increasingly reported.
- In school year 2023–2024:
  - **708 dossiers** involving psychological violence were recorded in primary education.
  - More than half involved prolonged or severe bullying.

### Secondary Education (Voortgezet Onderwijs)

- Approximately **57,000 students** in secondary education are bullied annually.
- Bullying victims lose an estimated **~3 hours per week** in concentration, recovery time, absenteeism, or educational participation.
- In 2023–2024:
  - **375 psychological violence dossiers** were registered in secondary education.
  - **202 cases** specifically involved serious bullying.

---

## National Growth in Safety Reports

The Dutch Education Inspectorate reports continued growth in complaints and incident reports:

| School Year | Total Dossiers |
|---|---:|
| 2021–2022 | 1,743 |
| 2022–2023 | 2,152 |
| 2023–2024 | 2,317 |

Additional indicators:

- Unique reports increased from **3,125 → 3,657**
- June 2024 recorded a historic peak of **529 reports in one month**
- Psychological violence is the largest and fastest-growing category

---

## Workplace Bullying Impact

Outside education, bullying also creates major economic impact:

- Estimated labor productivity loss per workplace bullying case:
  - **~€45,000 per case**
- Costs include:
  - absenteeism
  - burnout
  - replacement
  - productivity loss
  - healthcare costs

This is increasingly used in Dutch prevention campaigns to justify early intervention and social-emotional education.

# Structural Trends in the Netherlands

## 1. Increasing Psychosocial Pressure

Dutch education authorities report rising:

- anxiety
- social isolation
- conflict escalation
- discrimination reports
- online harassment
- severe bullying duration

Especially after COVID-era disruptions, schools report more persistent social safety problems.

---

## 2. Pressure on Social Infrastructure

Multiple organizations reference broader societal pressures:

- youth care waiting lists
- shortages in educational support staff
- teacher shortages
- reduced municipal social budgets
- overloaded welfare and prevention systems

These factors reduce schools’ preventive intervention capacity and increase escalation risk.

---

## 3. Citizenship Education Often Still Weak

The 2025 State of Education report concluded that citizenship education at many schools remains:

- insufficiently goal-oriented
- fragmented
- inconsistent
- weakly connected to student realities

Schools often lack:

- measurable objectives
- integrated curricula
- practical implementation strategies
- teacher confidence for difficult conversations

This is increasingly seen as directly related to bullying prevention and social safety.

## Resources for Teachers & Schools

### Government & Inspectorate Support

#### Onderwijsinspectie (Education Inspectorate)

Provides:

- reporting systems
- safety frameworks
- incident monitoring
- guidance on social safety obligations
- citizenship education supervision

### Expert Support for Citizenship Education

#### Expertisepunt Burgerschap

Offers:

- free advisors for schools
- policy guidance
- teacher professionalization
- curriculum support
- classroom methodologies
- helpdesk support

Schools can receive assistance after inspectorate findings or proactively improve social cohesion and democratic education.

Website:
https://www.expertisepuntburgerschap.nl

---

### School Safety Support

#### Stichting School & Veiligheid

Provides:

- anti-bullying protocols
- crisis support
- classroom interventions
- online safety programs
- social safety training

Website:
https://www.schoolenveiligheid.nl

---

### Teacher Development Needs

Teachers increasingly report:

- “handelingsverlegenheid” (lack of confidence in intervention)
- difficulty facilitating polarizing discussions
- uncertainty around citizenship implementation
- emotional overload from social conflicts

Professionalization demand is rising significantly.

## Municipal Youth Support

Dutch municipalities (“gemeenten”) increasingly provide:

- youth social workers
- neighborhood teams (“wijkteams”)
- school social workers

However, access varies strongly by municipality and waiting lists remain a major issue nationally.

# Emerging Dutch Policy Direction

## Increasing Focus Areas

Dutch educational policy is increasingly emphasizing:

### Social Safety

- earlier intervention
- mandatory monitoring
- escalation reporting

### Citizenship Education

- democratic resilience
- media literacy
- polarization prevention
- respectful dialogue

### Whole-School Approaches

Schools are encouraged to integrate:

- classroom culture
- teacher training
- parental involvement
- student participation
- behavioral policy

rather than treating bullying as isolated incidents.

---

# Overall Conclusion

The Dutch data suggests that bullying and psychosocial safety issues are not isolated educational problems but broader societal stress indicators.

The strongest observed trends are:

1. Rising bullying and psychological violence reports
2. Increasing severity and duration of incidents
3. Growing pressure on schools to provide social stabilization
4. Insufficient preventive capacity due to staffing and budget pressures
5. Expanding governmental focus on citizenship education and social cohesion

At the same time, the Netherlands has developed a relatively strong ecosystem of public and nonprofit support resources for:
- teachers,
- students,
- parents,
- and school leadership.

The main challenge now appears to be implementation capacity rather than lack of awareness or policy direction.
